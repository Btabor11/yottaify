Four investigations run while the agent worked. Nothing here edited a source
file; all of it is findings you apply when the tree is quiet.

Severity: **A** = fix before launch · **B** = should fix · **C** = note.

# 1. Privacy policy vs. what the site now actually collects

**This is the most serious thing in this document.** It is a legal exposure,
not a copy nit, and it appeared in the last few hours of agent work.

`content/legal.ts` currently states:

> "Only what you type into the reservation form: *[form fields]*. Nothing else
> is requested and nothing is inferred from a third-party data broker."
>
> "If analytics is enabled, we record page views and a single event when a
> reservation is submitted successfully."

Both sentences are now false.

## A1 — Server-side collection the policy does not mention

`lib/server/request.ts` attaches to every submission:

| Field | Note |
|---|---|
| `ipHash` | salted SHA-256 of the IP — **the raw address is computed but never persisted**; verified against every write path in `lib/server/` |
| `userAgent` | up to 400 characters |
| `country`, `region`, `city` | from Vercel geo headers |
| `deviceClass` | inferred — mobile / tablet / desktop / bot |

`lib/server/context.ts` additionally captures, per reservation: `path`,
`referrer`, `landingPath`, `utmSource`, `utmMedium`, `utmCampaign`, `utmTerm`,
`utmContent`, `locale`, `timezone`, `viewportW/H`, `screenW/H`.

Screen dimensions, timezone, locale and user-agent together are a
browser-fingerprinting surface. Combined with city-level geo and a work email,
this is personal data under GDPR and CCPA — not "only what you type."

*Correction to the first draft of this section:* the raw IP is **not** stored.
`requestFacts()` computes it, but only the salted hash reaches a column, and
the schema comment says so explicitly. The hash salt, however, defaults to
`"dev-salt-not-for-production"` when `IP_HASH_SALT` is unset — see LAUNCH.md.

**Also unmentioned:** the follow-up form (`lib/server/context.ts`,
`followupSchema`) stores **phone number**, role, team size, current provider,
current spend, compliance requirements and dealbreakers. None appear in the
policy.

## A2 — The journey record is behavioural profiling, and says so

`lib/journey.ts`, verbatim:

> "Everything here is attached to the reservation row so the person reading it
> knows which page the lead landed on, what they read, whether they ran the
> estimator and with what numbers, and how long they took over the form."

That is a reasonable thing to build. It is not "page views and a single
event." The event list in `lib/analytics.ts` includes `section_view`,
`page_leave` with dwell time in ms, `outbound_click` with the destination href,
`faq_open`, `estimator_change` with the GPU and hour values, `cta_click` and
`pricing_source_click`.

The `firstparty` provider's own comment says it exists to "join *what did they
read* to *did they reserve*." Written down plainly, that is exactly what the
policy tells visitors is not happening.

## A3 — "Have it deleted" contradicts the schema

Privacy, *Your choices*: email us "to have it deleted."

`lib/server/schema.ts:15`: **"Never updated, never deleted."**

No retention or purge logic exists anywhere in `lib/server/` — the only
`delete` calls are rate-limit bucket eviction and a foreign-key cascade. The
policy also admits "retention has not been set," so the page simultaneously
promises deletion and says the retention question is open, against a schema
designed to be append-only.

## A4 — LinkedIn ads make this concrete, not theoretical

`content/legal.ts` says these pages exist because "LinkedIn lead-gen ads reject
destinations without a reachable privacy policy." So there is planned paid
acquisition, UTM capture is already wired for it, and LinkedIn will send EU and
UK traffic. A materially inaccurate privacy notice on an ad destination is the
version of this problem that actually gets noticed.

## What to do — DONE, pending wiring

`content/data-inventory.ts` now exists. It describes all 70 columns of the
reservations table and is typed `satisfies Record<keyof NewReservation, …>`,
so a column added to the schema without a description fails typecheck. It
exports `PRIVACY_DRAFT`, generated from the inventory. To adopt when the tree
is quiet: `export const PRIVACY = PRIVACY_DRAFT;` in `content/legal.ts`, and
`export * from "./data-inventory";` in `content/index.ts`.

Two decisions are rendered honestly as open until made: `RETENTION` (days per
category, currently `null` → "not yet set") and `DELETION.implemented`
(currently `false` → the policy does not promise deletion).

The original recommendation, for the record — do not hand-edit these paragraphs — the file's own header says "replace
`sections` wholesale with counsel's copy." Instead:

1. **Generate the collection list from the code, the way `collectedFields`
   already derives form fields from `FIELDS`.** Export the context and request
   field names and render them into the policy. Then the policy cannot drift
   from the schema again, which is the same discipline the price table uses.
2. **Fix the two false sentences now**, before counsel, because they are live
   and wrong: replace "only what you type" with form fields *plus* technical
   and campaign data, and replace "page views and a single event" with an
   honest description of the journey record.
3. **Decide retention and implement it**, or remove the deletion promise. A
   promise you have no code path to honour is worse than silence.
4. `lib/analytics.ts` references **`DATA.md`, which does not exist.** Either
   write it or drop the reference.

There is a real opportunity here. A site whose entire thesis is sourced,
dated honesty could publish a genuinely complete data inventory — every field,
why it is kept, how long. Almost nobody does. It would be on-brand, and it is
mostly a matter of printing what the schema already knows.

# 2. Price survey refresh — the floor has moved

Checked 4 Sep 2026 across getdeploying, gpufinder, gpus.io, gpucost,
thundercompute, computeunion, plus provider pages.

## A5 — The $7.89 verified-in-stock floor no longer holds

Three independent trackers now show in-stock B300 below it:

| Source | Cheapest shown in stock | Note |
|---|---|---|
| gpus.io | **Lium $7.00** | marked available; "8.8% decrease over 30 days" |
| gpufinder | **Nebius $7.85** | uk-south1, hourly stock check |
| getdeploying | $7.89 | RunPod and GPU.ai; median also $7.89 |
| gpus.io | Verda $7.50 | marked available |

Below-rate listings also exist that the page does not name: **Prime Intellect
$4.99**, **Vast.ai $5.63**, **Bentaus $6.50**.

The argument survives — $6.75 is still under every *bookable* rate found — but
the margin is now ~3.6% against Lium's $7.00, not the ~17% the $7.89 figure
implies. Move the number, or the first buyer who checks finds a better one.

## A6 — The aggregators contradict each other, which is a better argument

Same GPU, same day, three trackers:

- RunPod: **$6.94** (gpufinder, out of stock) · **$7.39** (gpucost) ·
  **$7.89** (getdeploying, in stock)
- Nebius: **$7.85 in stock** (gpufinder) · "not reported" (getdeploying)

This is stronger evidence for the thesis than anything currently on the page.
The claim "published B300 prices are unreliable" is usually an assertion. Here
it is demonstrable: name the three trackers, show the three numbers, date them.
That is a paragraph only this site would write.

## B1 — The stat the page is missing

getdeploying publishes: **"14 of 90 configs in stock"** across 28 providers.

15.6% of listed B300 configurations are actually purchasable. That is a
third-party number, from a source with no stake in the argument, that says
precisely what the site spends several paragraphs asserting. It belongs in the
hero.

## C1 — Deploybase is stale and proves the point

`deploybase.ai` still describes B300 as largely unavailable with "expected"
Q2 2026 pricing — written around March 2026 and never updated, sitting on a
live commercial page. Same failure at **Hyperstack**, whose live B300
reservation page reads "coming to Hyperstack — On-Demand in August, reserved
private clusters in Q4" **with no year on it.**

A dated-citation discipline is hard to explain in the abstract. A screenshot of
an undated competitor promise is not.

## C2 — Spot and reserved rows to keep an eye on

Nebius spot $4.30 · CoreWeave spot $4.48 · Spheron spot $5.85 · Impossible
Cloud reserved 24mo **$5.16, in stock** · TheAI Cloud reserved $4.90–6.75.
The committed row's $4.25–5.62 range still holds. The spot caveat added
yesterday is now doing necessary work.

# 3. Competitor teardown — what buyers expect

Full detail from live pages at Nebius, CoreWeave, Together, Lambda, RunPod,
Hyperstack, Crusoe, Modal, Voltage Park.

## The gap that matters most

**B2 — Publish the inter-node fabric, in the hero.**

Every serious competitor publishes it: CoreWeave 800G XDR non-blocking
InfiniBand, Nebius Quantum-X800 at 800 Gb/s per GPU, Voltage Park 3200 Gbps,
Lambda Quantum-2 at 3.2 Tb/s aggregate.

Sixteen GPUs is two nodes, and **the only interesting technical question about
two nodes is whether they train as one.** If there is InfiniBand between them,
the switch model belongs next to the headline. If it is Ethernet, say
"single-node workloads; 16-GPU multi-node training is not supported on this
fabric" — a buyer finds out in hour one either way, and finding out after
paying converts an honest operator into a dishonest one in their memory.

## B3 — The spec sheet is the cheapest place to beat the field

CoreWeave publishes CPU SKU (dual Granite Rapids 6747P), 192 vCPUs, 4,096 GB
RAM, 61.44 TB local NVMe, **and the GPU driver version**. Hyperstack publishes
rack power draw (14.5 kW).

None of this needs a track record. It needs someone to walk into the warehouse
and read the labels. Missing line items to publish: CPU SKU, vCPU count,
system RAM, local NVMe capacity, driver version, rack power, and measured
round-trip latency to two named peering points.

## B4 — Lambda's own SKU boundary reframes "only 16"

Lambda's smallest 1-Click Cluster is **exactly 16 GPUs, at $9.86/GPU-hour**,
and their Blackwell Ultra clusters "scale from 16 GPUs to thousands."

So the entire fleet is precisely the industry-standard *smallest cluster unit* —
not a toy — and the closest name-brand equivalent charges $9.86 against $6.75.
"One Lambda-minimum cluster at 68% of Lambda's cluster rate" is a much better
sentence than "we have sixteen GPUs."

## B5 — Four majors will not sell a B300 hour without a phone call

CoreWeave publishes open on-demand pricing for B200 and H100 but **"Contact
sales" for B300**. Together publishes H100/H200/B200 and gates B300, GB200 and
GB300. Lambda advertises HGX B300 on its homepage and prices it nowhere.
Crusoe does not list B300 at all.

The lead-time argument does not need rhetoric. It needs a table of which
competitors will quote a B300 price without a call — checked and dated.

## B6 — Publish an SLA, because the majors do not

CoreWeave publishes 99.9% on *object storage only*, with no numeric
GPU-compute SLA. Lambda publishes none. Crusoe publishes none. Nebius makes
credits the "sole and exclusive remedy," future-use only.

**An SLA is a promise, not a record.** A pre-launch operator cannot claim
uptime history but can publish a contractual SLA with a real credit schedule on
day one — the only trust signal available that beats the majors while having
zero customers.

## What to refuse

- **No SOC 2 language of any kind** — not "in progress," not "aligned," not
  "enterprise-grade security." State: no third-party certification as of
  *[date]*, then describe the actual physical and tenancy facts, and name the
  workloads this is *not* appropriate for. Volunteering the disqualifying case
  is the highest-credibility move an unknown has.
- **No customer logos, no anonymous testimonials.** An empty space is more
  credible than a vague substitute.
- **No spot tier.** At 16 GPUs a spot tier means preempting the only other
  tenant. Saying that plainly is a better page element than a fake column.
- **No scale language.** "From one GPU to thousands" is a lie by implication at
  16. State "16 GPUs, two nodes" as a fixed fact.
- **No NVIDIA proximity badges.** Buying 16 GPUs from a reseller is not a
  partnership.
- **No per-instance pricing.** From a name brand it reads normal; from an
  unknown it reads as obfuscation.

## C3 — Sharpen the lead-time citation

The strongest public figures found are 36–52 weeks for B200 through standard
hyperscaler channels (GPUaaS, June 2026), with **no B300 lead-time data
published anywhere.** So "four to ten weeks" is *conservative*, which is the
right direction — but it needs a source you can stand behind. Your own dated
quote requests are better evidence than any blog, and the claim should read
"hyperscaler quote-to-provision for reserved capacity," not a bare interval.

# 4. Search demand — the honest verdict

**Organic search will not be a meaningful channel at launch, and likely not in
year one.** Three structural reasons:

1. The money queries — "B300 price per hour," "B300 cloud pricing," "cheapest
   B300" — return 8–9 programmatic aggregators out of 10. getdeploying updates
   daily and stamps "checked <15m ago"; gpus.io and computeunion run hourly
   scrapers. A hand-maintained table cannot out-fresh them, ever.
2. **There are 16 GPUs.** Even a wildly successful content program points
   demand at inventory that sells out with two or three customers. The
   constraint is capacity, not awareness.
3. Realistic time-to-rank on a new domain is 12–18 months, by which point B300
   is trailing-edge and the queries have moved to Rubin.

## B7 — Get listed in the aggregators. This is the actual channel.

getdeploying (28 providers), gpus.io, gpufinder, computeunion, computeprices,
gpuperhour, gpucloudprices. **Being a row in those tables is worth more than
any page you can write, and it is a form submission rather than an 18-month
content program.** They also carry the in-stock flag, which is the field that
converts — and "in stock" is the one column where a small operator with real
hardware beats the majors outright.

## B8 — The pricing page is competing with the wrong opponent, and losing

One URL is doing two incompatible jobs: showing *your* rate, and being a market
comparison table. As a comparison table it loses to daily scrapers on freshness
and coverage. Worse, it is conversion-hostile — its function is to show a
price-sensitive visitor that Prime Intellect charges $4.99.

Recommend splitting: `/pricing` for your rates and what is included, and
`/b300-pricing-comparison` for the sourced market table. The comparison page
then earns its keep as a citable asset — AI answer engines increasingly prefer
pages carrying provenance, and "sourced and dated" is the one thing getdeploying
does not offer.

## Recommended metadata

Current home title spends its first six characters on a zero-volume brand and
the rest on a slogan nobody searches. Suggested replacements:

**Home title** (56)
`Rent NVIDIA B300 Bare Metal — 8-GPU NVLink Nodes | Cluer`

**Home description** (152) — omits the price deliberately, since $6.75 loses a
naked comparison and the differentiator should land first:
`Dedicated NVIDIA B300 bare-metal nodes — 8 GPUs, 288GB HBM3e each, full NVLink, single tenant, no queue. Two nodes available in Arkansas from Nov 2026.`

**`/pricing` title** (50)
`B300 Bare Metal Pricing — $6.75/GPU-Hour On-Demand`

**`/b300-pricing-comparison` title** (56)
`NVIDIA B300 Price Per GPU-Hour: 2026 Provider Comparison`

**`/b300-pricing-comparison` description** (149) — naming competitors is what
the ranking pages do, and it earns long-tail coverage:
`Published B300 on-demand rates from AWS, RunPod, Vast.ai, Hyperstack and more — each with a source link and the date we checked it. Updated monthly.`

Keep the existing H1 — "sourced and dated" is a genuine differentiator against
scrapers that show a number with no provenance.

## C4 — Content worth writing, and what to skip

Worth it: `/b300-bare-metal-vs-virtualized` (thin SERP, matches "single tenant
B300"); an availability page (gpufinder built a whole heatmap because the intent
is real); **first-party benchmarks once the fleet is live** — the only
link-earning asset a no-authority domain can produce, because nobody can scrape
primary data.

Skip: "B300 vs H200/B200" pages — that is a programmatic arms race against
getdeploying's compare matrix. And skip anything geo-targeted at Arkansas;
nobody searches for GPUs by US state. Arkansas is a trust asset, not a keyword.
